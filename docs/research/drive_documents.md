## 0. Key facts at a glance

- **Project name:** *The Sound of Entanglement* (TSOE). The first realization carried the title *BruQner – The Sound of Entanglement*. German talk titles: "Der Klang der Verschränkung" and "Der Klang der Quanten-Verschränkung". The earliest working title, in the Nov 2022 application, was "BruQner – von der klassischen Musik zur Quantenphysik".
- **Website already referenced in press texts:** http://soundofentanglement.com/ (source: `2025 10 - CIVA Wien/Pressetext CIVA.docx`, `2025 11 - Zirkus des Wissens (JKU)/Ankündigungstext.docx`)
- **Format:** a "lecture-performance" (also written "performance lecture"). A roughly 20–25 minute popular physics lecture is followed by a roughly 20 minute live audiovisual performance, often with Q&A afterwards.
- **Core idea:** a live photonic Bell test acts as the "quantum conductor". Its entangled-photon measurement results, at stations "Alice" and "Bob", drive the music and the live visuals in real time. The measured Bell value is about S ≈ 2.45–2.7; the classical bound is 2.
- **Pieces:** *BruQner* (2024, two organs), *8 Rooms* (2025, band), and *Indeterminate Apparatus* (2025; its working title was "CIVAnewcomp").
- **Composer and musical director:** Clemens Wenger. His Palermo talk script says "So far, I've composed three pieces for our setup."

### 1c. Resolved discrepancies

1. **BruQner dates: 4 & 6 September 2024 (not 4 & 5).** Every source agrees on 4 and 6 September. These include the press release (Wed 4 Sept 22:00 English; Fri 6 Sept 16:00 German), the rehearsal timetable (Excel serials 45539 = 2024-09-04 "URAUFFÜHRUNG", 45541 = 2024-09-06 "2. AUFFÜHRUNG"), the OÖ Landespreis documents ("4. und 6. September 2024"), the SFB BeyondC slides, the Leonardo manuscript ("September 4th and 6th, 2024") and the Pint of Science and Berlin slides ("September 4th & 6th"). No document mentions 5 September.
2. **8 Rooms premiere: 25 January 2025.** The Vienna Ball of Sciences was on Saturday 25 Jan 2025 (ball schedule "25.01.2025"; tour notes "Saturday 25 January"; Leonardo "premiered on January 25, 2025"; past-performance lists "January 25th"; ball photo filenames `FRPIC_250125_BdW_...`). Two of the six sets (01:30 and 02:00) ran after midnight, so they fell on the early morning of 26 Jan. The production schedule shows "SO 26. Jänner" after midnight. The correct premiere date is 25 Jan 2025.
3. **Indeterminate Apparatus premiere: 3 Oct 2025 at CIVA** (not 2 Oct). The CIVA folder files are named "2025 10 02" and the tour notes say "02 & 03 October 2025". However, the venue production sheet (FS) puts the TSOE performance on **Friday 03.10.2025, 20:30–21:30**. The script header reads "3. Oktober 2025", and the past-performance list says "October 3rd". The 2 Oct date was presumably set-up or rehearsal.
4. **Audience at the premiere:** "über 3000" / "over 3,000" appears in the press, OÖ and CIVA texts. The SFB BeyondC slide says "3100 people in the cathedral (fire safety limit)".
5. **Zirkus des Wissens announcement text vs. what was played.** The announcement (`Ankündigungstext.docx`) reuses the 8 Rooms description ("per Quantum-Random-Walk durch virtuelle Räume") and calls Manu Mayr "E-Gitarre & Produzent". The actual script and slides for 19 Nov 2025 present **"Indeterminate Apparatus"** (Bell Metronome and VoQoder) with Manu Mayr on **bass** and Judith Schwarz on drums. Use the script's information.
6. **Osaka:** this was an exhibition presence (a video in the Austrian Pavilion, "composing the future", 13 Apr – 13 Oct 2025), not a live concert. The Kepler Award text (early 2025) still said "Weitere Aufführungen sind ... auf der Weltausstellung in Osaka ... geplant".
7. **Clemens Wenger's affiliation varies by date.** The ISQCMC abstract (spring 2025) gives the University of Music and Performing Arts Graz. The OÖ letter says "ab Oktober 2025 an der Anton Bruckner Privatuniversität Linz". The Leonardo manuscript (Sept 2025) gives the Institute for Composition, Conducting and Computer Music (IKD), Bruckner University Linz, "professor of composition ... specializing in jazz and eclectic music". **Use Bruckner University Linz for current copy.**

### 2.1 Project description: CIVA press text (the best current bilingual description)
Source: `2025 10 - CIVA Wien/Pressetext CIVA.docx`

**Header:** THE SOUND OF ENTANGLEMENT — Performance-Lecture, 40min + Q&A 20min

**DEUTSCH:**
> Die zweite Quantenrevolution als audiovisuelles Happening: Laser, Spiegel, nichtlineare Kristalle – ein Versuchsaufbau aus dem Hightech-Labor live auf der Bühne. Ein Team aus KünstlerInnen und WissenschaftlerInnen haben sich zum Ziel gesetzt, das quantenmechanische Phänomen der Verschränkung hörbar zu machen. Mit einem Laser werden verschränkte Paare von Lichtteilchen, sogenannte Photonen, erzeugt. Verschränkte Teilchen verlieren ihre individuellen Eigenschaften, haben aber eine besonders starke Beziehung zueinander, egal wie weit sie voneinander entfernt sind – Albert Einstein nannte dieses Phänomen „spukhafte Fernwirkung". Der Nobelpreis für Physik im Jahr 2022 wurde für Experimente verliehen, die dieses Verhalten demonstrierten. Heute ist die Verschränkung das Rückgrat moderner Informationstechnologien wie Quanteninformatik und Quantenkryptographie. Bei THE SOUND OF ENTANGLEMENT übernehmen die verschränkten Photonenpaare die Rolle eines Dirigenten: Die Messdaten werden in Musiknotation und synthetische Klänge übersetzt, die Musiker:innen müssen in real-time damit interagieren. In Verbindung mit Live-Visuals, die ebenfalls vom Quantenexperiment gesteuert werden, entsteht eine sinnliche Symbiose aus Musik, bildender Kunst und moderner Forschung. Der Performance steht eine kurze, allgemeinverständliche Physikvorlesung voran, die über das Phänomen der Verschränkung informiert und die Methode zeigt, wie das Experiment verwendet werden kann, um damit Kunst zu machen. THE SOUND OF ENTANGLEMENT wurde beim Festival Ars Electronica 2024 und antonbruckner2024 zum ersten Mal im Linzer Mariendom vor über 3000 Menschen präsentiert. Es folgten eine Einladung an die renommierte Johns Hopkins University in Washington, zum Wiener Ball der Wissenschaften und als Teil des Österreichischen Pavillons bei der EXPO2025 in Osaka, Japan.

**ENGLISH:**
> The second quantum revolution as an audiovisual spectacle: lasers, mirrors, nonlinear crystals—an experimental setup from a high-tech laboratory live on stage. A team of artists and scientists has set itself the goal of making the quantum mechanical phenomenon of entanglement audible.
> A laser is used to generate entangled pairs of light particles, known as photons. Entangled particles lose their individual properties but have a particularly strong relationship with each other, no matter how far apart they are – Albert Einstein called this phenomenon "spooky action at a distance." The Nobel Prize in Physics in 2022 was awarded for experiments that demonstrated this behavior. Today, entanglement is the backbone of modern information technologies such as quantum computing and quantum cryptography.
> In THE SOUND OF ENTANGLEMENT, the entangled photon pairs take on the role of a conductor: the measurement data is translated into musical notation and synthetic sounds, which the musicians must interact with in real time. In conjunction with live visuals, which are also controlled by the quantum experiment, a sensual symbiosis of music, visual art, and modern research is created. The performance is preceded by a short, easy-to-understand physics lecture that explains the phenomenon of entanglement and shows how the experiment can be used to create art.
> THE SOUND OF ENTANGLEMENT was presented for the first time at the Ars Electronica Festival 2024 and antonbruckner2024 in Linz Cathedral in front of over 3,000 people. This was followed by an invitation to the renowned Johns Hopkins University in Washington, to the Vienna Ball of Sciences, and as part of the Austrian Pavilion at EXPO2025 in Osaka, Japan.

> What does quantum entanglement sound like? "The Sound of Entanglement" is a groundbreaking synergy of art and science which transforms one of physics' most mysterious phenomena into a live audiovisual experience.

### 2.14 Physics explainer copy (EN; lecture script, lightly edited for web use)
Source: `2025 04 - Washington DC/Presentation/2025 04 14 - The Sound of Entanglement.docx`. The same text appears in the Alpbach, CIVA and BruQner scripts. German equivalents are in `TechForum Millstatt ... (deutsch).docx`, `2024 09 06 - Präsentation.docx` and `2025 11 19 - JKU Zirkus des Wissens.docx`.

- **Opening question:** "If a tree falls in the forest and no one is around to hear it, does it actually make a sound? ... What this question is really asking is if the tree's existence is dependent on being perceived. ... can we decide this question experimentally?"
- **Classical physics:** "The physics we knew around the year 1900 had four big pillars: ... Mechanics & Acoustics ... Optics ... Electrodynamics ... Thermodynamics. All those theories, which we now call classical physics, have common characteristics: Objects have well defined properties at all times; Everything has a cause; Randomness is only due to our ignorance of exact initial conditions; They are deterministic (even in chaos). Therefore, in classical physics, the universe is a deterministic clockwork."
- **Quantum mechanics / randomness:** "Quantum mechanics is indeed a radically different theory about the microscopic world. It breaks the deterministic clockwork. Some properties of microscopic systems are not definite at all times anymore. They can be in a superposition of different possibilities. ... In classical physics, seemingly random events – like the final position of a roulette ball – are in fact not really random. They are just too complicated to be calculated. ... We call this randomness subjective. In quantum mechanics, however, randomness seems to be objective. Whether a single photon – a particle of light – gets reflected or transmitted at a beam splitter ... is objectively random with 50:50 odds. There is no cause for the individual event."
- **Quantum revolutions:** "Quantum mechanics gave rise to the ... first quantum revolution ... the transistor ... Lasers ... Magnetic resonance imaging ... Today, we are living in the second quantum revolution. ... Information itself and the way we process it is quantum. We call these quantum information carriers quantum bits or qubits. ... a quantum computer from Innsbruck, Austria, built by the team of our colleague Martin Ringbauer, where the qubits are ions in a vacuum chamber ... a satellite-based quantum internet."
- **Entanglement:** "In our classical world, any pair of objects – for example two scores of music – is fully described by describing the two individual objects. ... In the quantum world, we can have Entanglement. The state of two entangled quantum systems cannot be described by fully describing the individual systems. The whole is more than the sum of its parts. This is a complete break with our classical world. There is no analogy for it. Schrödinger called entanglement 'the characteristic trait of quantum mechanics, the one that enforces its entire departure from classical lines of thought'. ... our experimental setup will create pairs of entangled photons via a process called down-conversion. Photons from a laser hit a special type of crystal. For every photon, there is a small chance that it splits into a pair of two photons traveling along two cones of light. If we select them properly, photons A and B are entangled in their polarization. ... Locally, measurement outcomes are completely random. The polarization does not exist before measurement. However, globally, the two outcomes are perfectly correlated, even if the photons are measured at distant locations."
- **Correlated music / quantum conductor:** "Entanglement is about correlations. Our world, our life is also about correlations. ... Music is about correlations and coordination. A conductor coordinates and correlates musicians. The conductor interacts with the musicians through light. ... In a pitch-black room, nobody can see the commands, so they would be meaningless. Tonight though, a special type of conductor takes over ... Entangled pairs of photons therefore are quantum conductors for our music. Physicists and computer scientists alike call the two stations Alice and Bob."
- **Einstein and EPR:** "Einstein ... did not believe in objective randomness and famously said that he is convinced that god 'does not play dice' ... He called [entanglement] 'spooky action at a distance' ... In 1935, Einstein and his colleagues Podolsky and Rosen put forward a very interesting argument that quantum mechanics is incomplete. They believed that we are missing so-called 'hidden variables' ... Just as the working of a steam engine can be reduced to the movement of all the water molecules in the steam, so could the random events in quantum physics be reduced to yet-unknown and hidden variables."
- **Bell's inequality:** "For almost 30 years, this remained an open problem ... It was John Bell, who in 1964 came up with an idea to decide this almost philosophical question experimentally. In a so-called Bell test, we have a source which sends out instructions. In our case, this is the conductor. And then we have two parties, in our case Alice and Bob ... Alice can choose between two measurement settings a1 and a2. And Bob can choose between b1 and b2. The possible measurement outcomes are called +1 and –1. ... There are 4 possible events for Alice ... 4 possibilities for Bob. Hence, there are 4 times 4 = 16 possible outcomes for every photon pair. ... a combination of these 16 probabilities ... are bounded by a certain value, namely 2. And this is Bell's inequality."
- **Bell violation:** "So, a classical conductor – bound by the laws of classical physics – cannot beat this Bell value of 2. But a quantum conductor can! Using entangled states, the correlations can reach a value larger than 2. [We will reach about 2.5 / 2.7 today.] The violation of Bell's inequality has been experimentally confirmed time and time again. Hidden variables are ruled out. The world is quantum. The 2022 Nobel prize in physics was awarded for some of the groundbreaking Bell experiments using photons to Alain Aspect, John Clauser, and Anton Zeilinger. ... This means, you will hear music which no human conductor (or classical computer) could instruct."
- **Fun fact:** "our very own Johannes [Kofler] here has contributed immensely to one of Anton Zeilinger's Nobel prize experiments. It was him, who got the theory and numbers right!" (DC and Alpbach scripts)
- **Bruckner–Boltzmann anecdote:** "Ludwig Boltzmann ... grew up in the 1850s in Linz and who else do you think was also in Linz at that time? ... Anton Bruckner and he was actually his piano teacher." (BruQner scripts)
- **Alpbach–Schrödinger link:** "Some of you might know that Schrödinger spent his final years here in Alpbach. So you may wish to visit his grave where also his famous equation is enshrined." (Alpbach script)
- **Setup (short):** "It starts with a laser, which using a special crystal, produces entangled photon pairs (here in red and blue). These entangled photons are guided over mirrors and half-wave plates to Alice and Bob, where they are guided, via fiber optic cables, to single photon detectors and measured. The orientation of each half-wave plate chooses between setting 1 and 2. There are 4 outcome options for Alice and 4 for Bob, and in total 16 possible combinations."
- **Technical setup (Leonardo manuscript):** built at TU Wien. Polarization-entangled photon pairs come from spontaneous parametric down-conversion: 405 nm laser photons become 810 nm pairs in a nonlinear crystal (the DPG slides give a β-BBO crystal, SPDC type-I). Bell state Φ+ = (|HH⟩ + |VV⟩)/√2. Motorized half-wave plates switch the measurement basis in under 100 ms; one full cycle takes about 400 ms. Polarizing beam splitters, fiber coupling and single-photon avalanche diodes follow. A time tagger (resolution under 350 ps) uses a 1 ns coincidence window. The measurement rate is about 2 Hz (DPG). The software chain is Python daemon → OSC over Ethernet → Cycling '74 Max patch (music cues and virtual instruments) plus a TouchDesigner network (visuals at 30 fps or more). The CHSH bound is S ≤ 2, the quantum maximum 2√2. The authors state that the setup does not close loopholes, which have been closed elsewhere.

## 3. The pieces

### 3a. *BruQner* (2024), for two church organs, experiment and live visuals
- **Composer:** Clemens Wenger, after Anton Bruckner. Registered at AKM as "Clemens Wenger and Anton Bruckner, 'BruQner,' akm, ISWC: 3307761800" (Leonardo ref. 39). The ISWC is quoted exactly as given in the manuscript; it looks unusual and should be verified.
- **Source material:** Bruckner's *Präludium in C-Dur für Orgel ("Perger Präludium"), WAB 129* (1884). The performance opened with the original *Perger Präludium* in the dark Dom. It ended with "Bruckner's PERGER PRÄLUDIUM in an extended version by Franz Neuhofer".
- **Instrumentation:** two organs in the Linz Mariendom. **Alice = Gerhard Raab at the Rudigier organ** (main organ, at the back). **Bob = Wolfgang Kreuzhuber at the choir organ** ("Chororgel", side organ, at the front). The two organs sat about 30 m apart. Sheet music was generated live on tablets with a 1-bar offset; LAN cabling gave 1–2 ms latency, where WiFi gave 1–15 ms.
- **How it works:** each measurement makes the quantum conductor pick one of 4 precomposed motifs for Alice and one of 4 for Bob. There are **28 motif sets**, giving **448 combinations**; the Berlin and old decks estimate "approximately 400000 options" over the global form. The DPG deck says "Over 200 precomposed bars" and "8 sections, 6 subsections, 250 precomposed bars of music chosen by exp". Clemens wrote the software over about 15 months ("An original software, written over the last 15 months by yours truly, turns this Bell Test into sheet music"). The mapping was designed so that non-classical correlations mostly produce polyrhythmic motif combinations.
- **Form, per the premiere lecture script:** "We have 7 short sections. The first 3 will be conducted by our entangled photons. In section 4 we switch between entangled photons and a classical physics setup. ... In Part 5 ... the two organists will take time to improvise freely without any instructions given. That is a bow to human free will and to Anton Bruckner, who was known as one of the greatest improvisers on the organ ... In the last 2 sections, we return to quantum world, and the photons will guide us back to the end with Bruckner's PERGER PRÄLUDIUM in an extended version by Franz Neuhofer." The DPG slide lists the sections as 1, 2, 3, 4-Q, 4-C, 5-C, 6, 7-Q, 8-C, with "Circles – Entanglement, Lines – Classical".
- **Duration:** "The premiere performance lasted about 18 minutes" (Leonardo); the performance segment is billed as about 20 min.
- **Measured Bell value:** S ≈ 2.45 at the premiere. A classical middle section gave S < 2.
- **Visuals (Enar de Dios Rodríguez):** two light cones, inspired by the SPDC emission cones, were projected into haze. Three laser projectors faced the audience ("the audience is sitting in the visual projection"). The cones were visible in the quantum sections; flat planes, or lines and dots, marked the classical sections. Leonardo text: "The cones of light did not simply illustrate entanglement; they embodied it – interacting, overlapping, and surrounding the audience like living sculptures."
- **Leonardo description (verbatim):** "The vision behind The Sound of Entanglement was first realized in a special composition called BruQner, on September 4th and 6th, 2024, in the New Cathedral in Linz, Austria as part of the Anton Bruckner 2024 and Ars Electronica festival. The music was played live on two organs, which we respectively call Alice and Bob. For each measurement, the quantum conductor ... chose from a set of eight precomposed musical motifs: four motifs for Alice and four for Bob. The thematic material for the sheet music is based on Anton Bruckner's organ piece Perger Präludium. This 'open-form' approach is characteristic of aleatoric music. ... In BruQner, the choice of motif has been left to the quantum conductor."
- **Program-note style excerpt from the premiere script:** "These motives are derived out of Bruckner's PERGER PRÄLUDIUM; some bars are completely identical with the original work, but there are many variations of Bruckner's ideas to develop the composition." From the Berlin deck: "...many variations, inversions, transpositions and rhythmic intensification of Bruckners harmonies to develop the composition as it progresses."
- **Media:** audio `2024 09 - BruQner/Visuals - Linz/Sound/BruQner_Composition_v6_teil1bis4b.mp3`; visuals `Linz01…Linz07_08_horizontal.mp4`; performance video `Fotos Aufführung/BruQner_Performance_Video.mp4`; trailer `Video/BruQner - The Sound of Entanglement (Trailer, 2025)(1).mp4`; previews `Preview Video/Bruqner_3_min.mp4`, `Doku/BruQner_Teaser_short.mp4`; `2026 05 - Pint of Science/Bruckner_Original_soe.mp4`.

### 3b. *8 Rooms* (2025), for synthesizer/electric piano, electric bass (and drums), experiment and live visuals
- **Composers and credits:** "Clemens Wenger, Manu Mayr, and Judith Schwarz, '8 Rooms,' akm (2025)" (Leonardo ref. 44).
- **Premiere:** 25 Jan 2025, Vienna Ball of Sciences, Vienna City Hall. Later performances: Washington DC (14 Apr 2025), Millstatt (3 Jun 2025), Alpbach (26 Aug 2025). A simulated excerpt was shown in the Palermo talk (Oct 2025).
- **Line-up:** Ball, DC and Millstatt used a duo of Clemens Wenger (synthesizer) and Manu Mayr (electric bass). Alpbach was a trio with Judith Schwarz (drums). The Munich slide says "Alice = Synthesizer, Bob = E-Gitarre" and the Zirkus text says "E-Gitarre"; both are inconsistent with every other source, which gives electric bass.
- **How it works (Leonardo, verbatim excerpts):** "The subsequent composition 8 Rooms also uses a Bell setup, but deviates from BruQner as it employs different methods to map the experimental data onto musical parameters. The basis of this composition is (i) digital audio processing of live sound that is reactive to the measurements and (ii) a 2-dimensional quantum random walk on a periodic square grid that controls the musical form. ... The main element of the composition is a stereo clicking sound generated by the rotation of the half-wave plates, i.e. coming directly from the setup. ... We timed these measurements to happen every 513 ms, enabling us to use these rotation clicks as a metronome (513 ms is 117 bpm in musical terms) and as a percussion element. ... As the name of the composition, 8 Rooms, suggests, there are eight different sections, or 'rooms'. ... Switching rooms happens when the random walk, which starts at the room's center, hits one of the four boundaries of a square. Each boundary forms a door to a different room ... After a preset duration, all boundaries are redirected to room 9, where the performance ends."
- **Tempo:** the tempo figure varies by source. Leonardo gives 513 ms, which it equates to 117 bpm. The slides say "approximately 114 beats per minute" (Munich, Berlin, Palermo). The DPG slides say "2 Hz = 120 BPM" and "Vienna Ball of Sciences – 2025, 120 BPM".
- **Program-note style text (DC/Alpbach script, verbatim):** "The main element of the composition is a stereo clicking sound, on the left side a click for ALICE, on the right for BOB. Picked up by two microphones, this sound comes directly from our experiment, it is generated by the rotation of the half wave plates, and each click sound comes from a single measurement when the angles of the Bell test are changed and the plates have to move mechanically. We timed these measurements, so we can use these rotation clicks as a musical metronome and percussion element. ... For each photon at Alice's side, one out of 4 outcomes is realized, A1+, A1-, A2+ or A2-. For each of the possible outcomes, a specific echo response is generated by the click. ... as the name of the work '8 ROOMS' suggests, the composition will move through a geometric plane of 8 ROOMS, each one sounding completely different, the clicking sounds of ALICE and BOB will always be manipulated by the experiment, sometimes sounding like distorted electric guitars, sometime sounding like a knock on a wood, and so on… ... We are using a so-called random walk that leads us stochastically through the rooms. Of course, this is not your classical random walk, but a very strangely behaving quantum random walk that shows a strong will to reintroduce some order into chaos. A quick explanation: A random walk is like a game of snake. The walk starts in the middle of the room and each measurement defines the path, until the snake comes to a border, which is a door to another room. After 19 minutes, all doors will be locked and the next exit will be the end of our composition, in the ninth room. ... over the total performance time of approximately 20 minutes we get more options than there are atoms in our universe. It is an impossible coincidence, if we repeated our performance, that you'll hear the same piece twice."
- **Alpbach addition:** "on the sides we will display the Bell value currently achieved by the quantum conductor. A small orange line indicates the classical bound, and all values above this line show that our performance operates in the quantum regime."
- **Palermo summary (three layers):** 1) stereo clicks from the half-wave plates, processed live ("short needle transients triggering complementary delay responses ... bitcrushed synth guitars"); 2) a correlated quantum random walk through 8 parts ("each side produces a locally random walk, but when put together globally, some order is reintroduced and the axes of our walk are skewed"); 3) the VoQoder.
- **Visuals (Leonardo):** "When the piece begins in silence, only two two-dimensional shapes – constructed from fine white lines on a black background, one representing Alice and the other Bob – are visible. As measurement events accumulate, these forms gradually transform into an undulating terrain ... generating a three-dimensional landscape. In this way the histogram becomes a spatial, poetic expression of the rhythmic structure that arises from quantum uncertainty." The spectra come from contact microphones on the rotation stages; each room (R1–R8) has its own camera angle and aesthetic.
- **Duration:** about 20 min in lecture-performances (all doors lock after about 19 min). At the Ball the sets were 15 minutes each. The YouTube video "Live at Vienna Science Ball" runs 11:43.
- **Media:** `2025 04 - Washington DC/Presentation/Media/8 ROOMS - clicking motives example A2-.wav`, `... B2+.wav`, `... correlation.wav`; YouTube https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Smz7cGPBODw.

### 3c. *Indeterminate Apparatus* (2025). Working title "CIVAnewcomp"; for synthesizers/electric piano (VoQoder), electric bass, drums, experiment and visuals
- **Premiere:** 3 Oct 2025, CIVA Festival, Belvedere 21, Vienna. Second performance: 19 Nov 2025, Zirkus des Wissens, JKU Linz. The title appears as "Indeterminate Apparatus" in the JKU and Palermo decks; the Munich deck misspells it "Indeterminate Appartus". At CIVA it was introduced as "so new that we haven't named it yet, so for now we call it CIVAnewcomp". The title comes from the script line "our metronome is a truly indeterminate apparatus".
- **Line-up:** Clemens Wenger (synthesizers, electric piano, MIDI-keyboard "VoQoder"), Manu Mayr (bass), Judith Schwarz (drums). Visuals by Enar de Dios Rodríguez.
- **Structure and duration:** "three 7-minute sections", about 20 min in total. Section 1 is quantum mode (S ≈ 2.5 → 90 bpm). Section 2 is classical mode (S ≈ 1.7 → 70 bpm). Section 3 is quantum again (→ 110 bpm). The mapping range runs from S = 0 → 40 bpm to S = 4 → 280 bpm (slides).
- **Program note (CIVA script, verbatim):**
  > The piece has two main ideas: **Bell Metronome.** We use this apparatus to determine the tempo of our piece; therefore, it is a metronome. However, unlike a traditional metronome ... where you set a tempo and rely on the beat, our metronome is a truly indeterminate apparatus. What we are doing is using the S-value of the bell inequality to map onto beats per minute (BPM). ... When we start measuring the entangled photons and calculating the S-value continuously, we initially get a lot of noise and the S-value fluctuates heavily between 0 and 4 - the extreme possible outcomes of the equation. This noise is fundamental. So, at the start of the piece, you'll find it very hard to grasp a stable tempo ... However, the more correlations we detect, the better the signal-to-noise ratio becomes, and after some time the S-value stabilises at the accuracy of our experiment. You will then hear that the tempo of our piece becomes more stable, enabling us to play rhythmic music using our Bell Metronome.
  > You've heard about the classical bound of the S-value of 2.0 ... but there's a higher limit for quantum: around 2.8 ... This is an important compositional element of our piece, which consists of three 7-minute sections. In the first section, our experiment will operate in quantum mode and we'll reach an S-value of around 2.5, which maps to a tempo of 90 bpm. In the second section, however, we manipulate our experiment so that it cannot see quantum correlations and operates in classical mode. That's why the metronome can only reach a maximum of 2.0; in our case, we'll reach around 1.7, which translates to a tempo of 70 bpm. So you'll be able to hear the clear difference between classical and quantum modes. In the third section, we switch back to quantum mode and accelerate to 110 bpm until the end of the piece.
  > Fortunately, you won't have to listen to the boring metronome sound during the performance, since we - the musicans - will follow the beat visually on our screens. Manu and Judith's main responsibility in this piece is to follow this uncertain metronome. So, each part will start in an unpredictable way, with a lot of noise and uncertainty in the tempo. However, after some time as noise is reduced, they will be able to interpret the correlations as musical time and perform their written drums and bass parts.
  > **VoQoder.** ... It's a new instrument that I imagined, which I call the VoQoder. I have split the keyboard in two: the left hand controls synthesiser 1, which we call Alice; the right hand controls synthesiser 2, which we call Bob. So, while I play the keyboard, the measurement outcomes from Alice and Bob alter the sound quality of their respective synthesizers. I choose the notes and pitches; the experiment chooses the sound settings, so I don't know the exact results. ... Now enter the Vocoder Circuit invented in 1930s for speech encoding. Vocoding only produces sound when both sides are played simultaneously. In the vocoder, the two synths, Alice and Bob, act as the carrier and modulator, modulating each other to produce an encoded synthesised sound. ...
  > This VOQODER method where every measurement can produce 512 different sound processings, combined with the BELL METRONOME which conducts us stochastically through the piece, adds up to hundreds of possible musical scenarios ... over the total performance time of approximately 20 minutes we get more options than there are atoms in our universe.
  > **Visuals:** ... they are programmed to be synchronized to the live measurements happening at the experiment, as well as the music being played. Conceptually, the S-value also shapes the visual experience, when chaotic, the pixels of the image appear disconnected, behaving in a disordered manner. As the S-value normalizes, and the tempo stabilizes, a clearer image will come into view. Furthermore, the protagonists of the quantum parts are very abstract motives, while the protagonist of the classical part is a very concrete and realistic object.
- **German version:** in `2025 11 - Zirkus des Wissens (JKU)/2025 11 19 - JKU Zirkus des Wissens.docx` ("Bell-Metronom", "VoQoder", "Dieses Stück ist so neu ...").

TouchDesigner files `CIVA-all.3.toe` and `Pixel-sorting.5.toe` (pixel sorting). Clemens's audio `new_steam_v2_90bpm.mpg`, `…_110bpm`. **ISQCMC 2025 abstract, "Investigating the Sound of Entanglement" (verbatim):**
Authors: Benjamin Orthner, Clemens Wenger, Johannes Kofler, Richard Küng, Enar de Dios Rodríguez, Martin Ringbauer, Alexander Ploier, Philipp Haslinger
> Quantum entanglement gives rise to correlations that no classical mechanism can replicate. **Clemens Wenger, long bio (DE, from `CV-Clemens Wenger.pdf`, about 2022):**
> Clemens Wenger, geboren 1982, lebt und arbeitet in Wien als Musiker und Komponist für zeitgenössische Jazz-, Pop- & Elektronische Musik. „Clemens Wenger ist längst ein Schlüsselfigur der jungen Wiener Szene (...)" schreibt Klaus Nüchtern im FALTER Portrait. Er hat die JazzWerkstatt Wien initiiert, leitet seit 18 Jahren dieses Musikerkollektiv und ist Kopf der erfolgreichen Wiener Soul Band 5/8erl in Ehr'n. Zuletzt hat Wenger „Physics of Beauty", eine Sammlung von 32 Stücken, als interaktives digitales Kunstwerk veröffentlicht. www.physicsofbeauty.art fand internationale Wertschätzung in Musik- und Designmedien, gewann den „Content Vienna" Hauptpreis und wurde beim Preis der deutschen Schallplattenkritik auf die Longlist 1/2021 in der Kategorie „Electronics/Experiental" gesetzt. Gemeinsam mit Mira Lu Kovacs veröffentlichte Wenger „The Urge of Night", wofür er spezielle Arrangements der Popsongs für ein zehnköpfiges Ensemble mit Hornquartett geschrieben hat. Am Münchner Volkstheater komponierte er u.a. den Soundtrack für die Erfolgsproduktion „Schöne Neue Welt", die - für die Bühnenmusik von Presse und Publikum gelobt - bereits in der vierten Saison gezeigt wird. Mit seiner Band 5/8erl in Ehr'n gewann Wenger 2021 zum sechsten Mal einen Amadeus Austrian Music Award.

Key CV facts: born in Krems a. d. Donau and based in Vienna since 2000. Studied jazz piano at the Gustav Mahler Konservatorium (with distinction), jazz theory and composition at the Konservatorium Wien Privatuniversität, and an MA in "Music for Film and Media" at Donau-Universität Krems (with distinction). Awards include several Amadeus Austrian Music Awards with 5/8erl in Ehr'n, Kulturpreis des Landes NÖ (Musik) 2019, Content Vienna Hauptpreis 2020, and Preis der deutschen Schallplattenkritik longlist 2021. He has taught at mdw (iPop) since 2017. Earlier science-related work: sound design for "Single molecules in a quantum interference" (Thomas Juffmann, Markus Arndt group, 2012), and "Start By Listening" at EFA 2022.

**Enar de Dios Rodríguez (from `CV-EnardeDios.pdf`, about 2023):** born 1986 in Spain; Vienna-based. MFA from the San Francisco Art Institute (2016); photography studies at the Universität für angewandte Kunst Wien. Exhibited internationally, including at Ars Electronica 2016, Kunsthaus Graz, LABoral and the Berkeley Art Museum. Chair of The Golden Pixel Cooperative. Co-founder of the art-science project SEEC Photography with Philipp Haslinger and Thomas Juffmann (published in *Leonardo*, 2017). Awarded the Marianne.von.Willemer-Preis in 2024 (per the Detaillierte Dokumentation). With Philipp Haslinger, she created "Temporal Forest" for the European Capital of Culture 2024 Salzkammergut.

Research: matter-wave and atom interferometry, electron–photon pairs, blackbody-radiation forces. Previously at UC Berkeley and the University of Vienna. Awards include the FWF START Prize (2018), EPS Young Scientist Prize (2019), Stanford Art of Science competition winner (2017) and the ASciNA Award (2016). Co-founder of SEEC Photography ("Photography at the speed of light"), shown at Ars Electronica 2016 and NHM Wien 2019.

**Richard Küng (from `CV-Richard Kueng.pdf`, about 2022):** Associate (now full) Professor for Quantum Computing at JKU Linz. PhD from the University of Cologne; MSc from ETH Zürich; postdoc at Caltech. Awards include the Kardinal Innitzer Prize 2022 and the Upper Austrian "Talentförderungsprämie für Wissenschaften" 2017.
