What you are looking at

Each pair of rings above is one pair of photons, measured during the premiere of BruQner in Linz Cathedral. The left ring is Alice's result, the right ring is Bob's.

A violet laser shines into a thin crystal. Very rarely, one of its photons splits into two infrared ones, entangled in their polarisation. One photon travels to Alice, the other to Bob, and each of them measures it along one of two directions, chosen at random every time. The answer is always +1 or −1, and on its own every answer is random.

Put side by side, the answers agree and disagree more strongly than anything obeying classical physics could arrange. The Bell value S measures that strength: any classical mechanism stays at or below 2, entangled photons can reach 2√2. The number under the rings is that value, computed from the pairs you have seen so far.

Red and blue rings mean Alice and Bob happened to choose the same setting; yellow and violet mean they chose different ones. The same colour on both sides means the same result.

In a performance this stream goes to the musicians and to the visuals as it happens. The experiment is the conductor, and no human conductor or classical computer can give the same instructions.

Three pieces

Every piece receives the same kind of data, one result for Alice and one for Bob, a few times a second. What differs is what the results are allowed to decide.

BruQner

2024, for two cathedral organs and entangled photons, after Anton Bruckner's Perger Präludium. Composed by Clemens Wenger.

Alice is the Rudigier organ at the back of the nave, played by Gerhard Raab. Each of her results picks one of four motifs for him to play.

Bob is the choir organ at the front, thirty metres away, played by Wolfgang Kreuzhuber, with four motifs of his own.

The motifs reach both organists on tablets one bar ahead, and are written so that entangled results mostly land on polyrhythms. In the middle of the piece we switch the experiment to classical correlations, and you can hear the difference.

8 Rooms

2025, for synthesizer, electric bass, drums and the clicking of the experiment. By Clemens Wenger, Manu Mayr and Judith Schwarz.

Alice is the click on the left. Every time her wave plate turns to a new setting, a contact microphone picks it up, and her result chooses one of four echo patterns for it.

Bob is the click on the right, with four echo patterns of his own. The clicks come about every half second, so they are also the metronome.

Together the results move a walker across a room: setting 1 moves it sideways, setting 2 up or down. When it reaches a wall, the band goes through that door into another room, each with its own sound. After about nineteen minutes every door leads to the ninth room, where the piece ends.

About 8 Rooms

Indeterminate Apparatus

2025, for synthesizers, electric bass, drums and entangled photons. Composed by Clemens Wenger.

Alice is the left hand on the VoQoder, a keyboard split in two. Her results change the sound of the first synthesizer.

Bob is the right hand and the second synthesizer. A vocoder only sounds while both hands play, the way a coincidence needs both photons.

The running Bell value sets the tempo. It starts noisy, then settles: around 90 bpm while the photons are entangled, around 70 when we turn the experiment to classical correlations, and 110 once they are entangled again.
