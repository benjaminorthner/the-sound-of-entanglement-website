About

Two photons are created together in a crystal, sent to two stations thirty centimetres apart, and measured. Each result on its own is pure chance. Together, the results are correlated more strongly than any classical system allows.

The Sound of Entanglement puts that experiment on stage and lets it conduct. A laser, a non-linear crystal, mirrors, wave plates and four single-photon detectors sit on a breadboard next to the musicians. About twice a second the experiment measures one entangled pair: a setting and an outcome at each of the two stations, called Alice and Bob. Those four numbers go straight to the composition software and to the visuals.

No two performances can be the same, and no human conductor or classical computer could produce the correlations you hear. We measure them live, in the room, and every performance reports its own Bell value: above 2 means the music was conducted by entanglement.

How the experiment works

Pieces

  1. BruQner2024. For two cathedral organs, after Anton Bruckner's Perger Präludium.

    Written for the Mariendom in Linz, one of the few churches with two organs that can play together. For every measurement the experiment chooses one of four precomposed motifs for each organist. The two players sat thirty metres apart and read their next bar from a tablet.

    In the middle of the piece the experiment is switched to classical correlations, and the motifs stop falling into the polyrhythms that entanglement favours.

  2. 8 Rooms2025. For synthesizer, electric bass and drums.

    The motors that turn the wave plates click at every measurement, and those clicks are the metronome. Each result sets off an echo pattern, one for Alice and one for Bob. Together the results steer a quantum random walk through eight rooms, each with its own sound. When the walk reaches a wall, a door opens into the next room.

    The map at the top of this page is that walk, running on the real measurements from the Ball night.

  3. Indeterminate Apparatus2025. For synthesizers, electric bass and drums.

    The Bell value sets the tempo. At the start it is all noise and the beat cannot settle. As correlations accumulate, the value steadies near 2.5 and the band locks in at 90 beats per minute.

    In the middle section the experiment is made classical. The value cannot pass 2, and the music slows to 70.
